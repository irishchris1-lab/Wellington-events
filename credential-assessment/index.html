Digital Credential Accreditation Self-Assessment

Two questions, answered in about fifteen minutes: should your credential be accredited under the Digital Identity Services Trust Framework, and are you ready to walk into a conversation with the GDDA?

What this tool does

New Zealand's Digital Identity Services Trust Framework Act 2023 came into force on 1 July 2024, with Regulations and Rules following in 2024. Accreditation under it is voluntary in law — but several doors, including issuing credentials into the Govt.nz app wallet, only open once you hold it. This tool helps you work out where you stand before you spend money finding out.

1

Should it be accredited?

Classifies what you operate against the five accreditable service types, tests whether your credential is a real credential, and weighs the drivers pushing you toward accreditation.

2

Are you ready for the GDDA?

Fifty-odd checks across the five Rules subjects plus DCIP technical onboarding. Produces a readiness score, a blocker list, and a prioritised set of next actions.

Who regulates it
The Trust Framework Authority, the regulator for accredited digital identity service providers.
What gets accredited
A service, not an organisation. One provider can hold accreditation for several services.
The five service types
Information, binding, credential, authentication and facilitation services.
The five Rules subjects
Identification management; privacy and confidentiality; security and risk; information and data management; sharing and facilitation.
Time to allow
About 15 minutes. Your answers stay in this browser — nothing is sent anywhere.
